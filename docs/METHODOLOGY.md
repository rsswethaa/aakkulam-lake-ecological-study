# Methodology

## Research question
How can structured field observations be used to explore possible relationships between urban pressure and ecological condition around a wetland environment?

## Observation design
A repeatable site-observation sheet records:
- water clarity score
- visible litter count
- vegetation-cover estimate
- odor score
- visible algae presence
- nearby urban-pressure category
- qualitative notes

## Sampling approach
For a real field study, observation points should be selected consistently across different edges of the lake and revisited over time. Time of day, recent weather and nearby activity should be recorded because they can influence observations.

## Scoring conventions
- Water clarity: 1 = very poor, 5 = very clear
- Odor: 0 = none, 5 = very strong
- Vegetation cover: estimated percentage from 0 to 100
- Urban pressure: low / medium / high based on visible development and human activity

## Analysis approach
The included script groups observations by urban-pressure category and compares mean litter, clarity, vegetation and odor. It also calculates algae-presence rates.

## Limitations
This portfolio reconstruction does not include laboratory water-quality measurements, seasonal sampling, GIS validation or the original academic field dataset. Observational scoring is inherently subjective unless supported by a detailed rubric and inter-rater checks.

## Ethical and reporting principle
Any future real-world version should clearly distinguish measured data, estimated observations and interpretation, and should avoid claiming causation from a small observational sample.
